Conservation Testing

A budget is only as trustworthy as the conservation of the quantities that feed it. This chapter sets out the step-by-step checks we use to test and cross-check that a SCHISM-AED budget is accurate — what can go wrong, how to benchmark each term, and what we have learned from applying the checks to the Pioneer runs.

The checks below are implemented as diagnostic scripts in analytics/pioneer_region_flux/diagnostics/. They target a run by name via run_config.py, so the same check runs against 002 or 003 without editing paths:

python diagnostics/conservation_check.py --run 003 --days 14

active_run() resolves --run / --run-dir / SCHISM_RUN (default 002) and --days; the RunConfig derives all run-file paths, parses the start date from param.nml, and reads n_regions from fluxflag.prop.

Problems and issues in mass conservation

Before trusting a budget it helps to know where conservation can break down in a coupled hydrodynamic–biogeochemical model. The recurring culprits are:

  • Source injection into small or dry cells. A point source adds volume and mass to its receiving element each step. If the injected volume is large relative to the cell volume — a high injection ratio \(\mathrm{rat}=Q\,\Delta t/\mathrm{bigv}\) — or the cell is intermittently dry, not all of the intended mass is retained.
  • Untracked boundary transport. flux.out only records transport across edges where adjacent fluxflag.prop values differ by exactly 1 (and neither is −1). Any control-volume perimeter that does not meet this rule is invisible to the boundary term.
  • Tidal cancellation. At a tidal gate the net transport is a small residual between large flood and ebb excursions, so sign and magnitude errors are easy to hide.
  • Coupling / numerical loss. In principle a tracer could be lost in the AED ↔︎ SCHISM hand-off or in transport; this must be ruled out before blaming the budget terms.

The remaining sections are the checks that isolate each of these.

Conservation benchmarking of AED variables

Theory. A conservative tracer (no sources, sinks or reactions inside the domain) must be conserved exactly bar boundary exchange. Comparing a tracer carried by AED against one carried natively by SCHISM, with identical forcing, isolates whether any loss is in the AED coupling or in the shared transport.

What we find. TRC_tr1 (AED) and GEN_1 (SCHISM-native) are injected identically and evolve bit-for-bit together — so transport is conservative and the AED hand-off is not losing mass. This is the decisive first benchmark: it moves attention away from the biogeochemistry and onto the source and boundary terms.

flux.out region transect assessment

Theory. The boundary term (E) is read entirely from flux.out at the control-volume transect — for the Pioneer CV, the (3↔︎2) mouth gate. Its accuracy depends on (i) whether the flagged transect actually covers the whole perimeter, (ii) the sign/direction convention, and (iii) how the raw positive/negative components are combined.

What we find. Classifying every edge around the perimeter shows that a large part of the mouth is untracked — transport leaves through edges flux.out never sees.

Reconstructing the flux directly from model velocity × layer thickness on each mouth edge, and comparing against flux.out, recovers the missing transport — confirming the gap is a fluxflag coverage issue, not a model error, and checking the directionality of the reported sign.

Looking at the raw gate signal shows why care is needed: the net is a small residual between large, near-cancelling flood/ebb excursions. Separating the positive and negative components keeps that explicit.

This tidal cancellation is also why run 003 holds the tide flat for the first 14 days (the flat-tide method): with the excursions removed, the source term can be assessed on its own.

Volume conservation

Theory. Before any tracer budget can be trusted, the control volume must conserve water: the change in CV water volume must equal what enters minus what leaves. With the control volume closed on the mouth transect, that is the realised river inflow plus the net volume flux across the mouth, \[\Delta V(t) = \int Q_\text{src}\,r(t)\,\alpha\,dt \;+\; \int q_\text{mouth}\,dt .\] Three subtleties matter. (i) The realised source differs from the raw vsource.th. SCHISM eases each source on over a startup window with the ramp \(r(t)=\tanh\!\big(2t/(86400\,\texttt{dramp\_ss})\big)\) (dramp_ss = 10 d), so the input is below the prescribed value for the first ~10 days; and a small empirical factor \(\alpha\) (--source-realisation-factor, default 0.96) captures a residual flow-proportional source-volume loss at the tiny (210 m²) src3 cell — the volume analogue of the small-cell rat-dilution (§Inflow source mass assessment). \(\alpha\) is applied only to the source term. (ii) Precipitation−evaporation would otherwise appear as a volume source, but run 003 sets isconsv = 0, so the precip/evap volume flux is switched off (schism_step.F90, the (precip-evap) term is inside an isconsv/=0 block) — it is not a term in this budget. (iii) The storage integral counts only actively-wet water: SCHISM’s wetting/drying scheme keeps a thin residual column (order h0 = 0.01 m) on cells it flags dry, and that numerically-dormant “dead water” would otherwise slosh in and out of \(\Delta V\) as the intertidal margin wets and dries. We exclude it by masking on SCHISM’s own dryFlagNode (zeroing dry nodes’ layer thicknesses in load_zcoord_dz), so the integral follows the model’s own wet/dry bookkeeping rather than re-deciding it.

What we find. Over the 14-day flat-tide window the budget closes to −0.5 % of throughput with the realised source (ramp × α = 0.96); the raw vsource.th leaves it ~28 % open, and ramp-only (α = 1) leaves ~−4 %. That ~−4 % ramp-only residual correlates with river inflow at r = −0.93 (≈4 % of the river volume) — i.e. it is a flow-proportional source loss, which is exactly what the small α corrects, and being source-side it propagates cleanly to river-borne tracer budgets via the known river concentration. Over the full ~53 days a residual of ~−9 % remains (−12.5 % with α = 1), and this part is real and accumulating — averaging out the tidal swing, the daily-mean residual trends monotonically through the tidal phase rather than oscillating about zero, and (unlike the flat-tide part) is river-uncorrelated (r = +0.07). α is deliberately not raised to force 53-day closure: that would mis-attribute the tidal mouth-flux error to the rivers.

It is, however, not a model volume leak: the whole-domain volume (total.out) conserves to +0.3 % on a tidal-mean basis (it oscillates by ±2.2×10⁹ m³ with the tide but its mean is unchanged). Nor is it dead-water over-counting: masking the ~17 % of node-samples SCHISM flags dry (subtlety iii) shifts the residual by <0.2 %, because that water is present at both ends and cancels in \(\Delta V\). The accumulation is instead a limitation of the CV budget under active tides: flux.out is a per-step velocity-based reconstruction of the transect flux, not bit-identical to the model’s internal semi-implicit continuity flux, and the CV volume itself swings by ±153 % of its mean each tidal cycle — so a sub-percent per-cycle mismatch between the diagnostic flux and the true continuity accumulates over ~70 cycles. With the tide held flat there is no such flux to accumulate, which is why the flat-tide window is the clean test and why run 003 was designed that way. The flat-tide closure (~96 %) confirms the control-volume geometry and mouth tracking are sound; the much larger salt and nitrogen gaps are tracer-side (source-injection loss, §Inflow source mass assessment), not a control-volume leak — but the tidal-phase volume residual is a genuine, if modest, accounting limitation, not numerical noise.

Confirming the cause — an independent mouth-flux reconstruction. To test whether the tidal-phase residual lies in the flux.out flux diagnostic rather than in the storage, the mouth volume flux was rebuilt independently from node velocities (horizontalVelX/Y) and the same zCoordinates layer thicknesses used by the storage integral (not ENV_layer_ht), summed over the 14 flag-2↔︎3 gate edges (edge_flux_zcoord_check.py). Over the flat-tide window the two agree to 0.1 % (−2.92 vs −2.92 ×10⁷ m³), so there the residual is unambiguously the source ramp, not the gate. Under active tides they diverge by ~33 % (flux.out −3.67 vs reconstruction −4.87 ×10⁷ m³), and the resulting volume residuals straddle zero — −12.7 % using flux.out, +15.1 % using the reconstruction (Figure 7). Two independent velocity-based estimates of the same gate bracketing closure from opposite sides is the signature of a flux-diagnostic limitation at a tidally-energetic, partially-wet gate: the true continuity flux lies between them and closes the budget. The storage term is exact and not implicated, consistent with the global +0.3 % volume conservation.

Inflow source mass assessment

Theory. A point source should add exactly \(Q\,c\,\Delta t\) of tracer mass per step. Whether it does depends on the receiving cell: a high injection ratio rat or a dry/near-dry cell means the model cannot accommodate the full injection and mass is under-applied.

What we find. At the Pioneer River source the pulse drives a mean \(\mathrm{rat}\approx 7\) in a shallow, intermittently dry cell, implying only a fraction of the intended mass is retained per step — a genuine source-side loss, separate from the boundary issue above.

Flow connectivity assessment

Theory. Source-cell problems are tied to the wider question of whether the upper river stays hydraulically connected. If the channel dries to disconnected pools over the tidal cycle, both the source injection and the transport of its load become unreliable.

What we find. The upper river is shallow and the wet channel narrows and widens with the tide, intermittently isolating the reach around the source — the context for the high rat above.

Theory. The previous checks focus on conservative quantities; the final step is to account for every state variable over the region — cumulative source input, tracked transect flux, and control-volume vs domain-integrated mass — side by side, so reactive variables can be sanity-checked too.
