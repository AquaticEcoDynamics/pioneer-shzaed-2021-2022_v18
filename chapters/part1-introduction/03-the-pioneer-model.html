Mackay Regional Domain

This chapter introduces the Mackay regional model — where it is and why it matters, the modelling approach, and the simulation scenarios maintained in this repository.

Location & setting

The model covers the Pioneer River estuary at Mackay, on the central Queensland coast of Australia, together with the adjacent coastal waters of the Coral Sea within the southern Great Barrier Reef lagoon. The Pioneer River drains a predominantly sugarcane catchment of roughly 1,500 km² and discharges through the city of Mackay to the sea.

Several features make this a demanding and interesting system to model:

  • Macro-tidal mouth. Mackay experiences one of the largest tidal ranges on the Australian east coast — spring ranges of around 6 m — so the estuary is strongly tide-dominated and the mouth exchanges large volumes each tide. (The boundary-forcing water levels span roughly 0.1–6.6 m, consistent with this range.)
  • Episodic, flood-driven freshwater. The climate is tropical with a pronounced summer wet season, and river flow is highly episodic — long dry spells punctuated by large floods. The most recent major event was the January 2023 Mackay flood, when the Pioneer River peaked near 2,600 m³ s⁻¹.
  • Catchment nutrient loads. The cane catchment delivers nutrient and sediment loads to a receiving environment adjacent to the Great Barrier Reef, so the estuary’s water quality and its exchange with the lagoon are a key management concern — and the motivation for coupling biogeochemistry to the hydrodynamics.

These characteristics — large tidal exchange at the mouth and sharp, flood-driven inputs at the river — are exactly the terms a nutrient budget must balance, and they shape the test scenarios described below.

Domain & approach

The model represents the Pioneer River estuary (Mackay, Queensland) and its adjacent coastal waters. The Pioneer River is the dominant freshwater input; it enters the model as a point source at the Bureau of Meteorology gauge 125013A (Dumbleton Pump Station) and drives the estuarine response during flow events.

The setup couples SCHISM hydrodynamics with AED biogeochemistry on a single unstructured mesh. River inflows enter as point sources (river forcing), the open ocean boundary is tidally forced (tidal forcing), the atmosphere is forced from gridded sflux fields, and biogeochemistry is configured through aed.nml. General conservative tracers (GEN) are included for mass-balance testing.

Simulation scenarios

The repository tracks a sequence of runs under runs/, each building on the previous one. They are recorded in runs/RUN_LOG.md and described in full in the run log chapter.

ID Name Purpose
001 Pioneer_17 Original baseline setup (archived, not in this repo).
002 002_P18_flood Pioneer 18 with an artificial flood pulse and a simplified fluxflag.prop to enable deep testing of the boundary-flux budget.
003 003_P18_flood_flat Copy of 002 with the flood pulse and a quiescent (flat) ocean tide for the first 14 days, to isolate flux-direction inconsistencies across the Pioneer mouth.

Each run directory holds only the essential input/config files; outputs are regenerated and are not stored in the repository.

Why these scenarios

The flood pulse and the flat-tide variant exist to support a specific investigation: a nutrient mass balance over the estuary would not close, and the two candidate causes — mass loss at the high-rate river source, and untracked transport across the tidal mouth — were entangled. Run 003 holds the tide flat for two weeks so the source behaviour can be examined without tidal exchange, then restores the full tide. The budget work is the subject of Part 4.
